NH3/H2 dataset and acquisition details
Sensor array
We use an array of 20 MEMS metal-oxide gas-sensor channels based on SnO2 nanowires with Pt/Ag surface modification. The 
sensors operate using a thermal-gradient sensing principle and provide complementary responses to NH3 and H2. The 
chamber accommodates the array in a 5 x 4 arrangement. We monitor the channels in four groups: CH1-CH5, CH6-CH10, CH11-
CH15 and CH16-CH20, with electrical supply settings in the range 3.0-3.4 V.
Gas chamber and delivery system
We deliver NH3 and H2 to the sensor chamber through an automated mass-flow-controlled mixing system. The apparatus 
comprises gas supplies, background-gas delivery, mass-flow controllers, a switching valve, the sensor chamber, a power supply 
and a computer for control and recording. The switching valve directs the prepared mixture to the chamber during exposure. 
The outlet is connected to an exhaust line, and a cleaning-gas flow supports recovery between exposures. Figure S2 shows the 
system layout; Fig. S3 shows the sensor hardware, chamber assembly and mass-flow-controller installation.
Acquisition protocol
We programmed single-gas and binary NH3/H2 conditions spanning 0–500 ppm for each gas, with nonzero settings of 25, 50, 
100, 200, 300, 400 and 500 ppm. Each sequence starts at gas delivery and records the complete 180-s exposure plus 700-s 
recovery cycle (880 s total). The acquisition interface was configured for a 2.0-s sampling interval. Acquisition took place over 
six days, but the six Combination-ID partitions were not intended as day splits. Day-by-day mixture details were not logged, 
preventing a reliable exposure-to-day mapping and leave-one-day-out evaluation. Temperature and relative humidity were 
neither controlled nor measured. These facts are clarified by the acquisition team; no timestamp-based cadence validation or 
retrospective environmental measurements are claimed.

Revision 3.0: additional analysis and protocol clarification
Acquisition independence and environmental limitations
Identifier separation and stored-signal fingerprint checks establish digital separation only. Acquisition days may be shared 
across evaluation roles; without exposure-to-day records, overlap cannot be quantified. The existing folds support held-out 
identifier evaluation, not verified day independence. Environmental variation cannot be quantified retrospectively. Future 

experiments will measure and record temperature and humidity; exposure-linked day/session logs are needed to enable day-
held-out validation.
Physical timing and exact window definitions
The configured interval is 2.0 s. Aggregate counts give 162,404/378 = 429.64 observations per 880-s sequence, corresponding 
approximately to 2.048 s per observation. This is consistent with the configured interval, but is not a timestamp measurement. 
A 64-sample window represents about 128 s nominally or 131 s by this aggregate calculation (71–73% of the 180-s exposure); 
the first-to-last timestamp span at nominal cadence is 63 intervals, or 126 s. The first sample marks gas-delivery onset.
For an L-sample sequence, with zero-based indices: early windows start at offsets 0–4; middle windows start at floor((L−68)/2) 
plus offsets 0–4; late windows start at L−68 plus offsets 0–4. Each has 64 consecutive samples. Tests use only offset zero. Full-
cycle windows linearly interpolate the complete interval [offset, L−1] to 64 points, using offsets 0–4 for pretraining/fitting and 
zero for testing. Full-cycle inputs include gas exposure and recovery. For approximately 430-sample cycles, middle and late 
windows predominantly probe recovery. Scalers are refitted using the corresponding pretraining windows; the primary early 
window remains predefined.
Multiplicity sensitivity
Table S9 retains equal fold weighting and independently resamples paired seed and fold axes. The 98.75% marginal percentile 
intervals implement a Bonferroni sensitivity for four comparisons using 200,000 draws (RNG seed 42). A second 200,000-draw 
run with RNG seed 2026 gives the same zero-inclusion decisions; endpoint shifts are at most 0.15 ppm. These are conditional 
descriptive bootstrap intervals. Bonferroni adjustment does not repair the limited number of chronological folds, overlapping 
training data, or unavailable acquisition-day labels. The original unadjusted Table I remains unchanged.

Loss denominator guard
Equation (14) guards both denominators. The V3.0 raw-response implementation adds clamp_min(1) to the visible 
denominator. With at most three spans of length 16 in a 64-sample window and at most three entirely hidden channels, at 
least 16 temporal positions remain visible in each nonhidden channel. The visible denominator is therefore always positive for 
the reported 16-/20-channel protocols. This defensive code change leaves their loss unchanged; historical predictions and 
results are retained.
