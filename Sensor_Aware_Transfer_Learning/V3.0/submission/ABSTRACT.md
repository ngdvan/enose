# Self-Supervised Transfer Learning for Label-Efficient Multigas Electronic Nose Calibration

Self-supervised transfer reduces mean absolute concentration error relative to training from scratch by 13.9% for NH3, 21.5% for H2, 27.5% for CO, and 25.7% for ethylene in our primary multigas electronic-nose evaluation. We pretrain a shared temporal encoder using masked sensor-response reconstruction, then fine-tune it with a limited set of labeled concentration pairs. The evaluation uses ten seeds, six held-out identifier partitions for NH3/H2, and three rolling chronological folds for CO/ethylene; the NH3/H2 results use a predefined early-response window. In secondary fixed-split experiments, fine-tuning with 10 labeled pairs gives approximately the NH3 and ethylene errors of scratch training with 20 pairs, a lower mean CO error, and a higher H2 error. These pair counts include validation labels. Sensor-identity and cross-sensor-attention ablations show target-dependent effects, with NH3 the weakest transfer case. In a known-gas-identity drift benchmark, transfer improves concentration regression in partitions 8 and 9 but not clearly in partition 10. The proposed model is not the best full-budget baseline on any of the four raw-response targets. The findings support target-dependent label efficiency for same-system calibration; the NH3/H2 identifier partitions do not establish between-day generalization.
