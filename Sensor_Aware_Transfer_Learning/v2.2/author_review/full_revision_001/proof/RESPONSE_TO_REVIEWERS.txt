Point-by-Point Response to Reviewers
The authors have completed the revised ten-seed experiment matrix, predefined fine-tuning as the primary policy, promoted 
multi-fold validation and added the requested controls. In the supplementary information, the authors describe their NH3/H2 
sensor array, gas chamber and acquisition protocol, with a system schematic and photographs of the experimental hardware. 
Yellow highlighting identifies revised or inserted manuscript content, not Word tracked changes. The manuscript is condensed 
to ten pages by removing repeated background and discussion. All four figures, ten main tables, 30 references and 
mathematical equations are retained; detailed seed sensitivity and generic-SSL comparisons remain in the supplementary 
tables.
Major comment 1
The premise is not tested. Pretraining reuses controlled exposures with labels withheld, so the labels already exist. The real cost 
in this field is generating the exposure, not annotating it. Either pretrain on genuinely unlabeled data (ambient, disjoint 
concentration regime, or a different array) or drop all language implying reduced calibration cost.
Response: The authors clarify that the research motivation is the expense of precise concentration control and labeling: 
mixtures without carefully controlled ppm can provide inexpensive unlabeled response recordings. The intended workflow 
pretrains on such recordings and reserves precise labels for a small calibration set. The revised Abstract, Introduction, problem 
formulation and Discussion preserve this motivation while identifying the present controlled-dataset experiments as a 
retrospective label-withholding test. They support label efficiency, but do not measure cost savings or demonstrate prospective 
learning from a cheaply acquired unquantified pool. The authors also distinguish the known-coordinate calibration-design 
control from label-free pretraining and from a total acquisition-cost budget.
Location: Abstract; Sections I, III.A, VI
Status: Addressed
Major comment 2
The "Best policy" column in Table I selects between frozen and fine-tuned transfer on the test partition (frozen for CO, fine-tuned 
elsewhere), contradicting the statement in Section V.B. All four abstract gains inherit this. Pre-register one policy, or select on 
the adaptation- only validation split, and recompute the abstract.
Response: Fine-tuning is predefined for every target. Scratch, frozen and fine-tuned models share a group-disjoint adaptation 
holdout; only adaptation validation selects checkpoints. The authors removed the test-selected summary and recomputed the 
primary abstract: NH3: scratch 31.01 → fine-tuned 26.69 ppm; reduction 13.9%; paired interval [0.38, 8.30] ppm; H2: scratch 
49.20 → fine-tuned 38.63 ppm; reduction 21.5%; paired interval [3.57, 18.78] ppm; CO: scratch 89.95 → fine-tuned 65.22 ppm; 
reduction 27.5%; paired interval [12.85, 37.53] ppm; Ethylene: scratch 2.611 → fine-tuned 1.939 ppm; reduction 25.7%; paired 
interval [0.247, 1.143] ppm.
Location: Abstract; Sections III.F, IV, V.B; Table I
Status: Addressed

Major comment 3
Three seeds is not enough. NH3 and H2 scratch and transfer distributions overlap at about one standard deviation, no 
hypothesis tests are given, and Table III has no error bars and is non-monotonic in several places (CO 20 vs 24 pairs, ethylene 5 
vs 10 pairs). Use at least 10 seeds with error bars on Tables III and IV.
Response: All principal configurations now contain the ten seeds 42, 142, …, 942, including seed-specific pretraining. Budget, 
baseline, ablation, window and drift tables report SD, and paired comparisons report seed/fold bootstrap intervals. The 1,680 
completed jobs retain predictions and identities. Seed accumulation does not show uniform erosion: first three → all ten 
primary reductions are NH3: 13.3% → 13.9%; H2: 18.4% → 21.5%; CO: 28.5% → 27.5%; Ethylene: 26.1% → 25.7%; however, 
secondary full-budget fixed-split gains attenuate from 32.4% to 19.6% (NH3), 27.2% to 23.3% (H2), 27.2% to 25.4% (CO), and 
31.1% to 24.3% (ethylene). Nonmonotonic budget behavior is retained.
Location: Sections IV, V.B–H; Tables I–X; Supplementary Table S2 and Fig. S1
Status: Addressed
Major comment 4
The multi-fold results in Section V.D are the most credible in the paper and are currently subordinated. They show smaller gains 
(16.4 percent for NH3, 14.5 percent for ethylene) and roughly double the absolute error, with an across-fold standard deviation 
of 17.81 ppm. The paired bootstrap intervals all exclude zero and are a defensible result. Promote this analysis to primary and 
demote the single-split tables.
Response: The primary quantitative result now leads with six NH3/H2 held-partition folds and three chronological CO/ethylene 
rolling folds. The authors replaced the historical results and its repeated-prediction bootstrap with equally weighted crossed 
seed-and-fold resampling. NH3: scratch 31.01 → fine-tuned 26.69 ppm; reduction 13.9%; paired interval [0.38, 8.30] ppm; H2: 
scratch 49.20 → fine-tuned 38.63 ppm; reduction 21.5%; paired interval [3.57, 18.78] ppm; CO: scratch 89.95 → fine-tuned 
65.22 ppm; reduction 27.5%; paired interval [12.85, 37.53] ppm; Ethylene: scratch 2.611 → fine-tuned 1.939 ppm; reduction 
25.7%; paired interval [0.247, 1.143] ppm. Intervals are conditional on observed exposures, and overlapping training partitions 
limit population inference.
Location: Abstract; Section V.B; Table I; Supplementary Table S1

Major comment 5
Drift partition 10 (146.95 plus or minus 8.71 against 142.78 plus or minus 1.13) is a null, not a 2.8 percent gain, and should be 
stated as such. This section also needs a constant- predictor control emitting the per-gas mean from the adaptation set.
Response: The authors added a per-gas constant fitted only to the adaptation fitting subset, with matched subsets across 
policies, all ten seeds, per-gas normalized errors and paired partition-specific intervals. Partition-10 scratch minus fine-tuned 
macro-MAE is -0.71 ppm, with paired seed interval [-8.40, 6.91] ppm. The interpretation follows this revised interval; the 
former small-gain claim is removed. The new protocol should not be conflated with the old unmatched-holdout three-repeat 
estimate.

Major comment 6
The sensor-aware claim needs a sensor-agnostic masked self-supervised baseline (for example TST, TS2Vec, or PatchTST). 
Internal ablations do not establish it. The feature representation given to Extra Trees and XGBoost is never stated, and a PLSR or 
SVR chemometric baseline is missing.
Response: The authors added a compact PatchTST-style masked, shared channel-independent Transformer without physical 
sensor-ID embeddings, evaluated at every fixed-split budget. Its departures from the forecasting configuration and differing 
capacity are stated. PLSR and RBF SVR use adaptation-only tuning; trees use flattened scaled windows (1,280/1,024 features). 
Full-budget paired generic comparisons are NH3: generic minus proposed -1.23 ppm [-2.46, -0.16]; H2: generic minus proposed 
3.58 ppm [1.53, 5.40]; CO: generic minus proposed 5.08 ppm [-2.31, 12.72]; Ethylene: generic minus proposed -0.189 ppm 
[-0.336, -0.034]. These mixed results explicitly limit the superiority claim.
Location: Sections II, IV, V.E; Table IV; Supplementary Tables S4, S4a, S8

Major comment 7
Equation (16) selects calibration pairs using the concentration labels it is meant to economize, so it is a design-of-experiments 
choice, not label selection. A random-selection control is also absent.
Response: Equation (16) is now described as coverage-based calibration-pair design using known concentrations. Random and 
farthest-point designs are crossed with scratch and fine-tuning at every budget and all ten seeds. Table III shows the five-pair 
experiment; Supplementary Table S3 reports every paired design contrast and interval. No claim of generic label acquisition 
remains.
Location: Section III.F; Section V.D; Table III; Supplementary Table S3
Status: Addressed
Major comment 8
Learned per-channel identity embeddings bind the encoder to one array and preclude cross- device transfer. State this 
limitation, and ideally test pretrain on array A, adapt on array B.
Response: The authors explicitly state that learned channel identities depend on array layout. The evidence concerns same-
system recalibration. A valid cross-device test needs compatible arrays and documented correspondence; no such matched 
acquisition is established here, so no cross-device performance is claimed.
Location: Sections III.C and VI
Status: Addressed by scope limitation; optional cross-device experiment not performed
Major comment 9
The private array is not characterized: no sensing material, operating temperature, sampling rate, baseline convention, or 
humidity and temperature conditions. Humidity in particular cannot be omitted for MOX sensors. Also, only about 68 of roughly 
430 samples per exposure are used, with no justification for the window placement, and the 0 to 500 ppm NH3 range does not 
match the environmental monitoring framing.

Response: The authors provide additional details of their NH3/H2 apparatus and acquisition protocol in Supplementary 
"NH3/H2 dataset and acquisition details". The authors describe the 20-channel MEMS SnO2 array with Pt/Ag modification, 
electrical supply settings, mass-flow-controlled gas delivery, switching valve, chamber and exhaust. Each programmed 
condition uses 180 s of exposure followed by 700 s of recovery, with a nominal 2.0-s sampling setting. Figure S2 presents the 
system schematic and Fig. S3 shows the sensor devices, chamber and mass-flow-controller installation. The existing window-
sensitivity analysis remains in Section V.F and Table VI. These additional apparatus and protocol details are supplied in the 
supplement; the main paper is unchanged.
Location: Supplementary "NH3/H2 dataset and acquisition details", Figs. S2-S3; existing Section V.F and Table VI

authors' array uses SnO2 with Pt/Ag modification, but array-level material information does not establish a chemistry-based 
causal explanation for the ablation differences. The authors therefore retain a cautious interpretation.
